Design Tokens Pro vs Subatomic

Subatomic: The Complete Guide To Design Tokens is the video course by Brad Frost and Ian Frost. It is the closest paid educational product to Design Tokens Pro, and it is excellent at what it sets out to do. It is also a different kind of product.

If you are choosing between a 13-hour workshop priced for teams and a written guide you can keep open next to Figma or a JSON file, the difference is format, depth of tooling walkthrough, and price — not whether tokens matter.

What each one is

Subatomic is a video course (13+ hours) with Figma and code sample architecture, naming and governance workflows, PDF slides, a private Slack channel, a certificate, and optional live team training. An individual license is $900. Two seats are $1,500; teams get 20% off per seat. It is aimed at organizations juggling many products, platforms, brands, and modes — Brad’s “Multi-All-The-Things” problem.

Design Tokens Pro is a written digital guide: eight chapters, 100+ pages, online access and a printable PDF, for $39. You can read it in almost any order; reading the whole thing takes about a couple of hours. It focuses on taxonomy, naming, architecture, compound tokens, themes, lifecycle, and documentation, with JSON examples that are not tied to one vendor’s file.

Where Subatomic is stronger

Choose Subatomic when the job is to train a team in someone else’s proven Figma + code architecture, with hours of recorded explanation, Slack, and a certificate. Brad and Ian have coached large organizations; the course includes sample systems you can fork, and a live training option if you need them in the room.

If you learn better from video than from text, or you need a shared artifact that procurement can treat as a course, Subatomic fits that budget and format.

Where the guide is stronger

Design Tokens Pro is built to be a reference you actually reopen. Naming conventions, token name elements, three-tier architecture, compound tokens, and documentation do not need 13 hours of playback — they need clear structure, examples, and a PDF you can annotate.

The guide stays useful when your stack is not Figma-plus-Style-Dictionary. Tokens show up in Penpot, Sketch, code-only repositories, and design tools that will change. The value is the mental model: how to name, layer, theme, and retire tokens so the system does not rot.

Price is part of the value. At $39 with a 30-day refund, the guide is a decision you can make without a training budget. Lifetime access includes future updates (a chapter on design tokens and AI is on the way).

They are not mutually exclusive

Plenty of people will want both: Subatomic to watch how an enterprise architecture is built in Figma and code, and Design Tokens Pro as the short written map of taxonomy and process. If you can only pick one, pick based on format and scale — video curriculum for a team rollout, written guide for a practitioner who needs to decide names and layers this week.
